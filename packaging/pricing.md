## Mega bundle

**ForgeKit Mega Bundle — $49.** All 10 products, one zip (`dist/forgekit-mega-bundle.zip`), one license.

Math for the listing: "$209 of tools for $49 — that's less than the price of any 3 products bought alone." Don't inflate this — $209 is the literal sum of the table above, so the claim holds up if a buyer checks.

## Launch pricing (first 72 hours)

Run each individual product $5 off its suggested price for the first 72 hours (e.g. ForgeNotes $19 → $14 launch price), and the bundle at **$39** instead of $49. Gumroad's built-in "sale price with end date" feature handles this — set it, don't hand-manage it.

After 72 hours, revert every price to the table above. Don't discount again for at least 30 days — repeat discounting trains buyers to wait for a sale.

## Tiering rule for future products

- Template/content packs (no code to run): $12–$19.
- Single-screen Streamlit/CLI tools: $19–$25.
- Tools with export/integration surface (CSV/Instantly export, n8n workflows): $24–$29.
- Never price a single ForgeKit product above $29 — it's a "ship today" impulse buy, not an enterprise sale. If a product feels like it's worth more than $29, it's a signal to split it into two products or fold it into a bundle instead.

## Bundle math discipline

Whenever a product's suggested price changes, re-sum the table and update the bundle pitch in `gumroad-listings.md` and `bundle-readme.md` to match. A bundle claim that doesn't check out against the individual prices is the fastest way to lose a refund dispute.

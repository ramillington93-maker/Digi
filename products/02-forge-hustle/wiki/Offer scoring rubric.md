# Offer scoring rubric

Use this to fill in the Confidence (1-5) column on the Offers database. Score honestly — a 5 you talked yourself into is worse than an honest 2.

## The five questions

Score each 0-1 (0 = no, 1 = yes). Add them up, then add 0 for a total score of 0-5. That's your Confidence number.

1. **Has someone already paid for this, or something close to it?** (Not "would they pay" — has money actually moved, from anyone, for anything similar.)
2. **Can you deliver the first version in under 10 hours?** If build time is higher, the offer needs to prove itself before you sink more time in.
3. **Is there a channel where your target customer already gathers?** (A subreddit, a Slack group, a LinkedIn niche.) No channel means no distribution plan, which means no offer.
4. **Is the price above $25?** Below that, you need volume to make it worth tracking. Volume plays belong in Testing status until proven, not Validated.
5. **Would you be embarrassed to charge for this in front of a stranger?** Score 0 if yes. This catches offers that are really just favors.

## What the score means

- **5** — Move to Validated. Raise the price 10-20% and see if demand holds.
- **3-4** — Testing. Run one more experiment before committing more build time.
- **1-2** — Idea, or reconsider the offer shape entirely.
- **0** — Kill it. Log why in the Notes column so you don't re-propose it in three months.

## Re-score monthly

Confidence is not a one-time score. An offer that scored a 4 in July because of one good Reddit post might score a 2 in August if that channel dried up. Re-run the rubric during your monthly review, not just when something feels off.
